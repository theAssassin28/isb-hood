===== Slide 16
Solution hypotheses
16
We believe [concept] will solve [problem] for [customer] while they are [doing this job].
SH1
We believe structured course reviews that require a reason and show professor, term and agreement will solve conflicting, reasonless, stale course advice for PGP students (and later YL students) while they are choosing electives and placing bids.
Wrong if: Under 20% of 30+ surveyed readers name a bid they changed; most still call 3 to 5 seniors
SH2
We believe track playbooks and company notes from people who made the same move, filterable by background will solve generic, late career advice for career-pivoting PGP students while they are planning prep and deciding on firms and offers.
Wrong if: Under 30% of 20+ career readers name a concrete action (applied, skipped, changed prep, offer choice)
SH3
We believe an ask-the-author comment thread on every entry will solve short senior calls and unanswered alumni messages for students with a doubt specific to them while they are acting on an entry.
Wrong if: Under 70% of questions answered within 48 hours
SH4
We believe a 5-minute structured form sent at term end and after placements, with credit will solve answering the same questions again and again for seniors and alumni who already help juniors while they are advising juniors.
Wrong if: Under 20 contributors or 40 reviews in 10 days
SH5
We believe the same entries, opened to the PGP YL cohort will solve no seniors in their own programme for PGP YL first-years while they are choosing courses and preparing for placements.
Wrong if: Under 30 YL sign-ups; YL readers find PGP entries irrelevant

===== Slide 19
MVP: what it is, why each part is in, what is minimum
19
One core loop, two tests run one after the other. Courses first; career a week later with its own pass line.
Test 1: Courses (primary)
Belief: alumni and seniors write course reviews; Co'27 changes a Term 7 bid because of one
Built: review form, course page, comments, sign-in, events
Pass: 40+ reviews from 20+ people; 20%+ of 30+ surveyed readers name a changed bid
Test 2: Career (starts a week later)
Belief: people who made a move write it down; a reader acts on it
Added: playbook and company-note forms on the same entry template, track and 'like me' filter
Pass: 10+ entries; 30%+ of 20+ readers name an action they took
Why test career now, not next term
Placement season is live; the same decision will not come back for Co'27
Co'27 and Co'26 career memories are freshest now; one outreach asks for both
Re-uses Test 1's form, page and comments: about 1 day of extra build
It starts only after Test 1 has 50%+ of its supply target, so course results come first and stay readable on their own
What is minimum about it
One entry template, two content types
Public comments only; chat and people finder are fake-door buttons that count clicks
One filter that matters per module
Moderation is us, by hand, from a flag list
No AI, places, events or mobile app
Hosted auth and database; seeded by our own outreach
Why 'one core loop' and not 'single feature': reviews, career entries, filters and comments are parts of one loop (write, read, ask). If any part is missing, we cannot tell whether the loop works.
MVP shape 05 from Session 4 (single feature, built properly). Full requirements in the separate MVP document.

How the counts were made
Each member coded their own transcripts (P, J, G, Q) and built an affinity diagram
We mapped each member's clusters onto 8 shared themes (slide 23); a mark means the theme was a main point for that person
Two members used Claude or Gemini for transcription and first-pass coding; clusters were checked against quotes
What the counts are not
Theme mentions in a mixed, non-random sample of 20 (13 PGP, 4 YL, 3 alumni), recruited through our own networks
Not population prevalence: '17 of 20' does not mean 85% of the batch has the problem
Interviewers sometimes pitched the idea first (noted by Auro); alumni speak about others, so their marks are weaker evidence
